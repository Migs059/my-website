PILLAR 01

Core Essentials

01.1

Password Hygiene

Weak or reused passwords are the easiest way in. Use a long, unique password for every account, and never repeat one across sites, since one breach shouldn't expose everything else. A password manager makes this easy by generating and remembering strong passwords for you.

Action: Deploy a password manager
01.2

Multi-Factor Authentication

A password alone isn't enough. MFA adds a second lock, like a code from your phone or an authenticator app, so a stolen password can't get an attacker in. Turn it on for every account that offers it, especially email and work accounts.

Action: Enable MFA everywhere
01.3

Patching and Updates

Updates fix security holes attackers already know how to exploit. Turn on automatic updates and don't ignore update prompts on your devices and apps. One unpatched device can be all it takes to compromise a whole network.

Action: Enable Auto-Updates
PILLAR 02

Everyday Habits

02.1

Phishing Awareness

Most attacks start with a simple email or message. Pause before clicking links or opening attachments from unexpected senders, and check the actual sender address, not just the name shown.

Action: Verify before clicking
02.2

Safe Browsing

Stick to sites that use HTTPS, and avoid downloading software or files from unofficial sources. If a site or download looks off, it probably is.

Action: Look for HTTPS & trusted domains
02.3

Device Locking

Lock your screen whenever you step away, even for a minute. Use a PIN, password, or biometric lock on every device you own.

Action: Win+L / Cmd+Control+Q on leave
PILLAR 03

Data Protection

03.1

Regular Backups

Back up important files on a regular schedule. Follow the 3-2-1 rule: three copies of your data, on two different types of storage, with one copy kept offsite.

Rule: 3 Copies / 2 Media / 1 Offsite
03.2

Data Classification

Know what counts as sensitive, like financial records, personal data, or login credentials, and handle it with extra care. Not all information needs the same level of protection.

Action: Label & restrict sensitive files
03.3

Encryption

Encrypt sensitive files and full drives, especially on laptops and mobile devices that could be lost or stolen. Encryption makes stolen data useless without the key.

Action: Turn on BitLocker / FileVault
PILLAR 04

Network and Access

04.1

Secure Wi-Fi

Avoid public Wi-Fi for anything sensitive. If you must use it, connect through a VPN to keep your traffic private.

Action: Always use VPN on public nets
04.2

Least Privilege Access

Only keep access to the systems and data your role actually requires. Fewer open doors means fewer ways in for an attacker.

Action: Audit permissions regularly
04.3

USB & Removable Media Caution

Never plug in a USB drive or external device you don't recognize. Unknown media is a classic way malware gets onto a system.

Action: Reject unknown hardware
PILLAR 05

Human Layer Risks

05.1

Social Engineering Awareness

Attackers often target people, not systems. Watch for impersonation, urgent pressure tactics, or someone trying to follow you into a secure area without badging in.

Action: Challenge unauthorized access
05.2

Social Media Oversharing

Details like travel plans, job specifics, or org structure can help an attacker craft a convincing scam. Think twice before posting them publicly.

Action: Limit public workplace details
05.3

Clean Desk Policy

Don't leave sensitive documents out or devices unlocked at your desk. A locked screen and a tidy desk close an easy gap.

Action: Clear desk & lock screen

Suspect an Incident or Security Breach?

Report suspicious activity immediately. Quick response minimizes potential damage.
